## 1. C1 — predictive-fitness routing for persistent JEPA adapters on a frozen visual world model

**Repo claim** (`ATLAS_proposal_v7.md` §2, "Primary — C1"): select among persistent adaptation modules by their *measured* action-conditioned rollout error on transitions none has trained on, rather than by similarity to a stored fingerprint. "To our knowledge no module-library method for frozen visual foundation models routes this way." Novelty is claimed *only* for the transfer of this mechanism to a frozen high-dimensional visual latent space with no calibrated likelihood — not for the mechanism itself (see §2 below).

**Search conducted:** two broad alphaXiv `discover_papers` sweeps (one keyword-anchored on "module library / adapter / frozen world model / routing / predictive fitness / rollout error", one on "continual world model / test-time adaptation / lifelong learning / adapter 2026"), both with `prioritize=recency`, one bounded to `published_after=2026-01-01`, plus deep reads of every paper that looked plausible.

**Candidates investigated and ruled out:**

- **HERA — Historical Evidence Routing Adapter** (arXiv:2608.05523, Aug 2026) `[VERIFIED, direct read]`. Routes *retrieved historical evidence/memory* (a structured patch-memory bank of past context tokens) into a **frozen V-JEPA-2** predictor via trainable cross-attention "Memory Registers" / "Workspace Registers," to fix physical-memory failures under occlusion (tested on IntPhys2). This is not a library of alternate *adaptation modules* selected by measured predictive fitness — it is a single always-on memory-retrieval adapter solving a different problem (recovering occluded object state, not adapting to a dynamics shift). **Does not threaten C1.**
- **EPM-JEPA — Operator-Side Experience Modulation in JEPA-Family World Models** (arXiv:2606.12979, Jun 2026, single-author, IIT Jodhpur) `[VERIFIED, direct read]`. Compares two mechanisms for injecting an experience-buffer summary into a **single** JEPA predictor under a gravity-shift on Moving MNIST: input-residual injection vs. LoRA-generated weight deltas (operator-side modulation). This is a *single continuously-modulated adapter*, not a persistent library of multiple discrete modules routed by measured rollout fitness — there is no library, no selection step, and no verification-gated commit. Its own headline result is a pre-registered **null result** (the two mechanisms don't differ by its own criterion) with a smaller, non-pre-registered secondary finding. Small-scale (GTX 1050 Ti, 4 GB, synthetic Moving MNIST), self-reported honestly as exploratory. **Does not threaten C1**, but is directly relevant background for item 2 below (AdaJEPA-adjacent extensions).
- **verdi — Retrieval Is Not Transfer for Continual World Model Optimization** (arXiv:2608.09537, Aug 2026) `[VERIFIED, direct read]`. An autonomous-research-agent framework that transfers *optimization strategies* (training/architecture repairs, e.g., "add REP/DINOv2 guidance") across **different pretrained world-model backbones** (Ctrl-World, Cosmos-Predict 2, RoboCoin) during offline development campaigns, verifying every retrieved strategy on the target backbone before accepting it into a shared memory ("Retrieval is not Transfer... becomes transferable knowledge only after target-side experimental validation"). This operates at the level of *whole-model training recipes across separate research projects*, not *per-episode adapter selection during deployment of one frozen model facing a non-stationary physics stream*. **Does not threaten C1** (different unit of action, different problem: offline meta-optimization vs. online deployment-time routing). Its "verify before transfer" principle is conceptually adjacent to C2 (see §3) but not scoped the same way — flagged there.
- Other papers surfaced by the sweeps (`Diagnosing JEPA World Models with Action-Conditioned Predictive Consistency` 2608.12939, `WorldSimProbe` 2608.09298, `Low-Rank Dynamics-Effective Latent Carriers...` 2608.15156, `LeFlow` 2608.24855, `World Action Models Enable Continual Imitation Learning with Recurrent Generative Replays` 2606.27374, `On the Identifiability of Controlled World Models` 2607.22430, `The World Model Remembers, the Actor Forgets: Dream Rehearsal for Continual Model-Based RL` 2607.19749) `[SECONDARY, title/abstract only]` — none of the abstracts describe a module-library-plus-measured-fitness-routing architecture for a frozen visual world model; they are diagnostic probes, generative-replay continual imitation learning (Dreamer-family, not frozen-JEPA), or unrelated. Not read in full; flagged here only in case a future pass wants to check them more carefully.

**Verdict: C1 — STILL NOVEL.** No paper found (pre- or post-April 2026) that routes among a *persistent library* of adaptation modules on a *frozen visual foundation-model world model* using *measured* rollout/prediction error on fresh transitions, in an online continual-deployment setting. **No threat found.** This is a real search across a meaningful slice of 2026 arXiv output, not a proof of absence — see Open Questions.

## 3. C2 — verification-gated expansion (commit a module only after demonstrating on future unseen data that it closes the deficit)

**Repo claim** (`ATLAS_proposal_v7.md` §2, "Secondary — C2", and §3.4's table): checked specifically against DEN, CN-DPM, Dynamic TMoE, ShiftEx, MBCD, CLARE. "To the best of our knowledge none verifies on future unseen data that an added module improves prediction before committing it."

I retrieved and read the actual expansion-trigger mechanism of all six papers directly (five via alphaXiv, MBCD via the local PDF since alphaXiv could not resolve it by title/author search).

| Paper | `[VERIFIED]` mechanism actually found | Verifies on future/unseen data before commit? |
|---|---|---|
| **DEN** (Yoon et al., ICLR 2018, local PDF confirms `arXiv:1708.01547`) | Selective retraining; if loss `L > τ` (a fixed threshold on the *current* task's own training loss), expand by `k` units per layer with group-sparsity regularization to prune back; a separate split/duplication step handles semantic drift. Algorithm 1/3, §3. | **No.** Expansion decision is a threshold on the current task's training loss, evaluated on data already used to trigger it — no held-out future verification step. |
| **CN-DPM** (Lee et al., ICLR 2020, `arXiv:2001.00689`) | Bayesian nonparametric (Dirichlet Process Mixture) responsibility `ρ_{K+1}` (generative likelihood under the base prior) exceeds a threshold ⇒ sample routed to a Short-Term Memory buffer; once the buffer of size `M` fills, a **new expert is created and trained immediately** on exactly that buffer's data ("sleep phase"), then added to the pool. §3.2, Algorithm 1. **Exact quote confirmed:** *"improving the accuracy of expert selection, which is the main bottleneck of our method"* (Conclusion) — matches the proposal's paraphrased quote about CN-DPM. | **No.** The buffer that triggers creation is the same buffer the new expert is trained and immediately deployed on — no future-unseen verification. |
| **Dynamic TMoE** (Zhu, Liu, Weng, Wu, `arXiv:2605.20678`, May 2026) | A "Temporal Memory Router" (GRU-based, not a plain memoryless router — slightly more specific than the proposal's plain "memory router" label) + an MMD-based Distribution Shift Detector: `D²_mmd > ε` (adaptive threshold) triggers the Evolvable Expert Manager to **immediately instantiate a new heterogeneous expert**, selected by a Drift Pattern Profiler, then pre-trained; pruning removes underused experts later. §3.4-3.6. | **No.** New expert is created and pre-trained at the moment of detection; no held-out verification step exists in the pipeline (Table 4's ablation confirms MMD-detection quality is compared against a simpler mean/variance detector, not against a verify-before-commit alternative). |
| **ShiftEx** (Bhope, Jayaram, Venkateswaran, Venkatasubramanian, `arXiv:2506.18789`, Jun 2025 — cited in proposal as arXiv:2506.18789, 2025, confirmed correct) | MMD-based covariate-shift detection at the party level; if no existing expert's latent-memory signature matches within threshold `ε`, a **new expert is created and Federated-trained immediately** on the triggering cohort (§5.2.4). Facility-location optimization (Eq. 2) balances match-cost vs. new-expert cost, but this optimizes *which* existing expert to reuse, not whether to hold out data before committing a new one. | **No.** New-expert training data is the same cohort that triggered its creation. |
| **MBCD** (Alegre, Bazzan, da Silva — see venue correction below) | Multivariate CUSUM (MCUSUM) on the log-likelihood ratio between the best-known context's Gaussian dynamics model and a fresh "unseen-context" alternative; when the novel-context statistic `W_new,t` crosses threshold `h`, **Algorithm 1 immediately** sets `z_t = new`, increments `K`, and initializes a fresh dynamics model + policy for the new context (Algorithm 1, lines 9-15) — no probation or held-out verification step exists before the new model starts being used and refined. §3-4. | **No.** |
| **CLARE** (Römer, Zhang, Li, Schoellig, `arXiv:2601.09512`, May 2026) | *More precisely characterized than the proposal's table entry.* Routing = autoencoder reconstruction-error argmin per layer (not raw "feature similarity" per se — it's per-layer autoencoder-discriminator reconstruction error). Expansion = a **z-score** of each layer's discriminator reconstruction error against running mean/std of prior tasks' errors; if all existing discriminators' z-scores exceed threshold `γ` on the *current* task's own training data `D_n`, a new adapter+discriminator is added and trained on `D_n` immediately (Algorithm 1, §IV-D). **Domain note:** CLARE is applied to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) *policy* models (flow-matching action generation on LIBERO + real robot arm), not to a world-model dynamics predictor with CEM planning. | **No.** Expansion decision and new-module training both use the current task's own data; no future-unseen verification. |

**Additional 2026 finding conceptually adjacent to C2, different domain:** **verdi** (`arXiv:2608.09537`, §1 above) explicitly frames its central design principle as *"retrieval is not transfer... a strategy validated on one [world] model is at best an optimization hypothesis for another, and becomes transferable knowledge only after target-side experimental validation"* — i.e., it verifies a candidate on **held-out target-side trials before accepting it into permanent memory**, exactly the *shape* of argument C2 makes. But the unit being verified is a whole-model optimization/training strategy transferred across separate research campaigns on different backbones (Ctrl-World, Cosmos, RoboCoin), not a deployment-time adaptation module for one model handling a non-stationary physics stream. This is worth citing in the paper as convergent 2026 evidence that "verify before commit/transfer" is an emerging design principle in world-model literature generally — but it does not scoop C2 as scoped.

**Verdict: C2 — STILL NOVEL** as narrowly scoped by the proposal (verification-gated expansion, evaluated on future unseen data, for a persistent adapter library on a deployed frozen model facing a non-stationary stream). The proposal's "to the best of our knowledge" hedge is empirically well supported by this check — all six comparison papers confirmed to commit immediately on the same data that triggered detection, with no future-hold-out verification gate. **No threat found.**

**Mischaracterization found (minor, fixable):** proposal's References section (`ATLAS_proposal_v7.md` §10) and body table (§3.4/§3.5) cite **MBCD as "ICML 2021"**. The actual paper (confirmed from the local PDF, `Papers/MBCD.pdf`) is *"Minimum-Delay Adaptation in Non-Stationary Reinforcement Learning via Online High-Confidence Change-Point Detection,"* Alegre, Bazzan, da Silva, **AAMAS 2021** (Proceedings of the 20th International Conference on Autonomous Agents and Multiagent Systems), arXiv:2105.09452 — not ICML. This is a venue error a reviewer who knows the paper would catch immediately. **Recommend fixing before submission.**

## 5. C3 — UMF validated against planning success, "addressing JEPA-WM's open appendix question"

**Repo claim** (`ATLAS_proposal_v7.md` §2, §3.2): "JEPA-WM reports that models which unroll many actions faithfully do not thereby succeed at planning, and asks in an appendix whether any proxy for success rate exists... Novelty claimed only for... validating [UMF] against planning success on public checkpoints, which addresses JEPA-WM's open appendix question."

**JEPA-WM itself** (Terver, Yang, Ponce, Bardes, LeCun, TMLR 2026, `arXiv:2512.24497`) `[VERIFIED, direct read]`. The proposal's factual claim that JEPA-WM has an appendix section literally titled **"Is there a proxy for success rate?"** (Appendix G.3) is **confirmed exactly**.

**However, the audit finds a framing nuance worth flagging.** JEPA-WM's Appendix G.3 does not leave the question fully open with no attempt — it runs a real empirical analysis: treating each training epoch of each of several model *variants* (WM, WM_W, WM-prop, WM-2-step, WM-L, etc.) as a data point, it computes Spearman correlation between validation-time embedding-prediction loss ("Vis Emb") and smoothed success rate, **and finds moderate-to-strong positive correlation** (e.g., Table 14 on Push-T: mean Spearman ≈0.82-0.86 across model variants; Table 15 on Wall: ≈0.70-0.81; weaker on Metaworld, ≈0.39-0.47). Its own conclusion is that embedding-space unrolling loss (their "planning objective," i.e., the same L2 quantity UMF normalizes) is "the metric most correlated with the success rate" among those tested, with real, stated caveats (OOD action sampling can corrupt the correlation; gradient-based planners can get stuck in local minima independent of model quality).

**Inference:** JEPA-WM's own appendix is best described as "a partial, empirical answer via a *between-model, across-training-epoch* correlation study" rather than "an open question with no attempted answer." Critically, this is a **different granularity** from what ATLAS's C3/RQ1 actually test: JEPA-WM compares different *model architectures/configurations* against each other over training; ATLAS's dissociation finding (N2, see §6) is about correlation **within one fixed arm at the episode level** vs. **which arm/chart is selected** — i.e., whether the metric can rank *candidate adapters for the same base model*, not whether it can rank *different base-model training runs*. This is a real and defensible distinction that the paper can lean on, but the current proposal text ("addressing JEPA-WM's open appendix question") somewhat overstates how open the question was left. **Recommend softening this sentence** to something like "extends JEPA-WM's appendix analysis (a between-model correlation across training epochs) to a new granularity — within-arm, episode-level validation for adapter selection — and finds a form of dissociation JEPA-WM's coarser methodology would not surface."

**Verdict: C3 — PARTIALLY OVERLAPPING.** The general empirical question (does embedding-space prediction loss correlate with success rate) has already been investigated by JEPA-WM itself with a real, moderately positive answer. ATLAS's narrower and more granular claim — that the *same* metric, applied within a chart-routing setting, predicts success *within* an arm but not *which arm is better* — is not directly tested by JEPA-WM and appears to be a genuinely new, more specific finding. But the "open question" framing needs softening; a reviewer who reads JEPA-WM's Appendix G.3 closely will notice it is not actually unaddressed.

## 6. N1/N2 — is the prediction-accuracy-vs-planning-success dissociation already established in the literature?

**This is the single most consequential question in this audit for how the paper must be framed.**

**Repo claim** (`ATLAS_SUMMARY.md` §4.1-4.2, `CLAIMS_MATRIX.md` N1/N2): a small adapter (`ln_act`) reduces the world model's latent prediction error (UMF) monotonically as training data increases (0.336→0.302→0.268 at 20/60/100 trajectories) but produces **zero** improvement in downstream CEM planning success (44.0% vs 43.0%, N=100 paired, 95% CI [-9,+7]), while UMF *does* correlate with success **within** a single arm (Kendall τ ≈ -0.4, p<1e-4, survives partial correlation controlling for difficulty). Described in `ATLAS_SUMMARY.md` as "the cleanest form of the dissociation result."

**Direct answer to the audit's mandated question, stated plainly: YES — the general phenomenon (a world model's prediction-accuracy metric can be dissociated from downstream planning/control performance) is well established in the literature, going back at least to 2020, and is currently an active area with multiple 2026 papers making structurally similar points in JEPA-specific settings. N1/N2, as a *general-phenomenon claim*, is a replication in a new setting, not a discovery.**

Evidence, in order of directness:

1. **Lambert, Amos, Yadan, Calandra — "Objective Mismatch in Model-based Reinforcement Learning"** (L4DC 2020, `arXiv:2002.04523`) `[VERIFIED, direct read]`. This is the foundational paper for the entire "objective mismatch" framing the proposal itself cites in its Section 3.2 caveat. Direct quotes: *"the likelihood of one-step ahead predictions is not always correlated with control performance"*; and empirically, on cartpole/half-cheetah with PETS (a classical, non-visual, non-JEPA MBRL setup), it shows near-zero-to-weak correlation between validation log-likelihood and episode reward across ~1000-2400 trained models (Fig. 3, Spearman-like ρ as low as -0.06 to 0.59 depending on dataset), and constructs an explicit adversarial demonstration (a model fine-tuned to keep log-likelihood high while reward drops from 176 to 98). This is **structurally the same finding as N1/N2** — a model-quality metric dissociates from control performance — just six years earlier, in a different (non-visual, non-JEPA) substrate. **This alone establishes that the dissociation as a general phenomenon type is not new.**
2. **Grimm, Barreto, Singh, Silver — "The Value Equivalence Principle for Model-Based Reinforcement Learning"** (NeurIPS 2020, `arXiv:2011.03506`) `[SECONDARY — title/abstract confirmed via alphaXiv discover_papers; not independently read this pass, but its content is described accurately and consistently by a paper I did read directly, see next item]`. Argues that models should be evaluated/trained by whether they preserve value-relevant structure, not by raw prediction accuracy — the theoretical companion to Lambert et al.'s empirical finding.
3. **"Operator-on-F complements value-equivalence: a planning-time diagnostic for latent world models"** (Vakalis, `arXiv:2607.04464`, Jul 2026) `[VERIFIED, direct read]`. Directly cites and builds on Grimm et al. 2020/2021. On a TD-MPC2 model-size sweep (cheetah-run), shows reward-prediction error stays in a narrow band `[0.028, 0.091]` across all five model sizes while planning return collapses to 0.9 at the 317M size — i.e., **a conventional model-quality metric (reward-prediction error) is silent to a real planning failure**, while their proposed "operator-on-F" diagnostic catches it (Spearman -0.90 with return). This is again the same *family* of finding (a metric people use to judge model quality doesn't track planning performance) applied to a different metric (reward/value-fit rather than latent prediction error) and a different substrate (TD-MPC2, not JEPA).
4. **"Predictive but Not Plannable: RC-aux for Latent World Models"** (Li, Li, Maeda, Ogawa, Haseyama, `arXiv:2605.07278`, May 2026) `[VERIFIED, direct read]` — this is the paper the proposal itself cites as "RC-aux". Its title states the dissociation almost verbatim: *"A latent world model may achieve accurate short-horizon prediction while still inducing a latent space that is poorly aligned with planning."* Its specific mechanism (temporal/spatial mismatch: short-horizon training vs. long-horizon open-loop planning queries; Euclidean latent proximity vs. finite-horizon reachability) is **different** from ATLAS's mechanism (a physics-regime shift degrades the CEM planner's cost ranking specifically, not a training/planning horizon mismatch inherent to the architecture) — so this is not the same finding, but it is the same *genre* of finding, in a JEPA-family latent-planning setting, published three months before ATLAS's own literature cutoff would need to account for it.
5. **"The Objective Is the Bottleneck: Latent World Models Encode What Their Planners Cannot Use"** (Joyjeet Singh, independent researcher, `arXiv:2608.12959`, Aug 2026) `[VERIFIED, direct read]` — see §7 below; also directly relevant here. Its headline finding — a CEM-planned JEPA-style world model (LeWorldModel, TwoRoom) whose predictor stays highly informative (imagined-state error only 19% of "assume nothing moves" even 3× further than the planner ever looks) while planning fails because the *planning objective* (squared latent distance), not the predictor, breaks down — is again "predictor fine, something else about planning breaks," the same structural shape as N1/N2/N3, in an extremely similar CEM+JEPA setup.

**My inference, stated plainly (not softened):** the specific claim "small adapter reduces prediction error monotonically with more data but produces zero improvement in downstream planning success" is very likely **not a discovery** in the sense of "no one has seen this shape of result before." What ATLAS can defensibly claim as novel is a **narrower, more specific instantiation**: (a) the substrate is a frozen visual foundation-model world model (DINOv2 + JEPA predictor) with an adapter library and CEM planning, not a from-scratch-trained low-dimensional MBRL model (Lambert et al.) or a from-scratch pixel/state JEPA (RC-aux, "Objective Is the Bottleneck"); (b) the dissociation is measured specifically as a function of adapter training-data budget (a 5× data sweep, N4) — this specific "more data helps the metric, buys nothing in success" sweep does not appear to have a direct precedent in the papers I found; and (c) the companion mechanism finding (N3, planner cost-ranking degrades specifically under a *dynamics regime shift*, while the model's own prediction error stays informative) is examined next.

**Verdict: N1/N2 — the *general* dissociation phenomenon is SCOOPED (well-established since 2020, actively repeated in 2025-2026 JEPA-specific work). The *specific instantiation* (frozen visual JEPA + adapter library + CEM planning + training-data-budget sweep) is STILL NOVEL as far as this search found, but must be framed explicitly as a replication-in-a-new-setting, not a discovery of the phenomenon type.** This is the single most important framing decision for the paper — see the closing section.

## 7. N3 — does the CEM/MPC planner's own cost ranking degrade under a dynamics shift specifically, while the model's own prediction error stays informative? Is that measurement new?

**Repo claim** (`ATLAS_SUMMARY.md` §4.3, `CLAIMS_MATRIX.md` N3): Spearman rho of predicted cost vs. true outcome, per-situation: +0.501 (95% CI [0.277, 0.726]) under default physics (R0), -0.072 (95% CI [-0.243, 0.099]) under a damping shift (R2); regret 8.5px (R0) vs. 88.1px (R2); under R2 the cost-ranked top-10 candidates are *worse* than the unranked batch mean.

**Closest prior work found: "The Objective Is the Bottleneck"** (`arXiv:2608.12959`) `[VERIFIED, direct read]`, discussed above. This paper demonstrates, in a strikingly similar CEM+JEPA setup, that the *planner's cost function*, not the predictor, is what breaks — quantitatively: "Cross-entropy-method planning minimises squared latent distance, which tracks true distance at r = 0.426, stops rising by about eighty arena units, and decreases beyond about a hundred and twenty — so moving away from the goal can lower the planner's cost." It reports a regret-like measurement too (§4.1's "26 of 37 failures finish farther from the goal than they began, at a median 1.41× the original separation") and a convergence-level ranking-quality result comparable in spirit to ATLAS's N3b (converged CEM plan lands farther than the start).

**The key difference (my inference, load-bearing for the novelty argument):** "Objective Is the Bottleneck"'s mechanism is a **geometric/horizon-based saturation-and-inversion** of the fixed cost function — it happens on the *same* physics, the *same* model, simply as a function of *goal distance*, and is present even absent any distribution shift. ATLAS's N3 mechanism is a **dynamics-regime-shift-specific** collapse of ranking quality — the *identical* planner/model pairing ranks candidates well under R0 (+0.501) and badly under R2 (-0.072), which is a claim about *shift sensitivity*, not about *horizon-dependent geometry*. These are genuinely different mechanisms even though both produce the same higher-level structural pattern ("planner ranking breaks while predictor accuracy stays informative").

I was not able to find any paper that specifically measures "CEM/MPC cost-ranking quality (Spearman ρ of predicted cost vs. true outcome) as a function of a physics/dynamics regime shift, holding the planner and model fixed, contrasted against a no-shift control" — which is exactly what ATLAS's R0-vs-R2 comparison does (`ATLAS_SUMMARY.md` §4.3's 2026-08-27 update). **This specific measurement — the regime-shift-conditioned ranking-quality comparison — appears to be genuinely new** as far as this search found, though it sits in the same emerging cluster of "planner-ranking-not-predictor-accuracy-is-the-real-bottleneck" papers (RC-aux, "Objective Is the Bottleneck," Operator-on-F) that a workshop reviewer familiar with this cluster would expect to be cited and distinguished from.

**Verdict: N3 — PARTIALLY OVERLAPPING** with "The Objective Is the Bottleneck" at the level of general finding-type (planner cost/ranking, not predictor accuracy, is the actual bottleneck in a CEM+JEPA system) — this paper should be cited and the paper's specific contribution (regime-shift-conditioned, not horizon-conditioned) should be stated explicitly to distinguish it. The specific dynamics-shift-conditioned ranking measurement itself is **STILL NOVEL** as far as this search could determine.

**A caveat on venue/rigor for both "Objective Is the Bottleneck" and EPM-JEPA:** both are apparently independent-researcher, non-peer-reviewed arXiv preprints (no stated venue, small compute budgets explicitly disclosed — a laptop CPU and a GTX 1050 Ti respectively), with very low visibility (1 and low vote counts on alphaXiv at the time of this search). They should be treated as real, dated, citable prior art (their claims are internally well-documented and specific, with real numbers and real ablations) but **not** as established, peer-reviewed consensus — a reviewer could reasonably discount their authority even while acknowledging they exist. This nuance matters for how strongly ATLAS needs to differentiate itself from them.
